What’s inside every site

This comes with every site we build, even if you can’t see it at a glance: we check it before handing your site over. It applies to every plan.

  • Checked before we hand it over. We run your site through a security checklist and a tool that reads it file by file, and we fix what turns up. It’s a check, not a guarantee: we look at the most common weak spots.
  • Easy to read and use for more people. We check that text has good contrast, photos have a description, everything works with just a keyboard, and the text can be enlarged (using a checklist based on the international web accessibility guidelines, WCAG 2.2 level AA). That way more people can use your site, including people who use a screen reader.
  • The basics for Google to understand you. Each page gets its own title and description with your trade and your town, your contact details in a format Google can read, and a site map. Nobody can promise first place: it also depends on your Google listing, your reviews, your competition and time.
  • Built for phones. It’s designed for phones first: buttons are easy to tap with a thumb, nothing gets cut off or slides sideways, and the WhatsApp button opens with your message already written. Photos are resized so they load faster.
  • In your name, with no trackers. Your domain and the account where your site lives are in your name and your email. The site has no trackers and no pieces from other sites that report every visit. And it stays online even if you don’t sign up for maintenance.
  • A guide to use it. You get your files in a .zip (they’re yours) and a short guide in plain words: how to protect your accounts, how to ask for changes and what to do if something breaks. If you ever want to switch providers, you can.
Technical details
  • Code: semantic HTML5, hand-written CSS (colors and sizes as variables in :root) and framework-free JavaScript (the base ships ~2.6 KB). No CDNs, no build step, no dependencies.
  • Hosting: Cloudflare Pages (free plan), on an account in the client’s name, with two-factor authentication on. Published by uploading the site folder. The domain is registered in the client’s name. HTTPS: after publishing we check that http:// and www redirect to https://.
  • Security headers: a _headers file with a strict Content-Security-Policy (no 'unsafe-inline' or 'unsafe-eval', frame-ancestors 'none'), X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin, Permissions-Policy and X-Frame-Options: DENY. The same CSP goes in a <meta> on every page. We check live that the real headers match _headers.
  • Security review: our own plain Node script, with no dependencies and no network access, reads the files and looks for secrets, inline scripts, resources from other domains, tracking code and missing headers. To hand over, it must exit with 0 critical and 0 high findings. Then a manual review by Security (the crew’s security agent), only on what the client authorizes in writing. It is not a penetration test: it doesn’t look at the live site, the account or DNS.
  • Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA checked by hand against a checklist: lang, a single h1 and headings in order, alt on images, 4.5:1 contrast (3:1 for large text), keyboard-only use with a visible focus ring, prefers-reduced-motion and 200% zoom. For mobile Lighthouse we aim for performance ≥ 90 and accessibility ≥ 95: those are targets, not measurements taken on clients.
  • Search: unique <title>, meta description and h1 per page, with trade and town; canonical, Open Graph (og:image 1200×630), sitemap.xml and robots.txt; LocalBusiness structured data (JSON-LD) using only details the client confirmed, checked at validator.schema.org. Google Business Profile: if the client already has one, we check that name, address and phone match the site.
  • Images and weight: WebP at ~1200 px, with width and height and loading="lazy" (except the first visible one). Targets: photo ≤ 200 KB and first load ≤ 500 KB; they are targets, not measurements taken on clients.
  • Cookies and tracking: no cookies and no trackers in the site’s files (checked on the base and the demos); no fonts, maps or videos embedded from other domains. That applies to the site’s files; what Cloudflare’s network adds on its own is outside our control.
  • Handover: the full folder in a .zip, a client guide, and a delivery checklist run in full (zero pending placeholders, photos with permission, photo and font credits).

    Before

    Requests by chat, calls and scraps of paper.

    After

    Every request reaches your email and lands in your spreadsheet.

    Technical details
    • Included in all three plans. If you ask for it on its own, it’s a one-time payment.
    • What it uses: a Google Sheets spreadsheet with a Google Apps Script, in your Google account. The site posts the form to that script’s exact URL. No server of ours.
    • Where the data lives: the sender’s name, email or phone and message land in the “Consultas” tab of your spreadsheet (your Drive) and in the alert sent to your inbox. They pass through Google, which is already your provider: no new third party is added. We can see the spreadsheet only while we hold editor access, and we remove it at handover (if you sign up for maintenance, for as long as it lasts).
    • Whose account: yours. You do the install with your own login, guided by us. Nobody asks for or writes down your password.
    • Third-party cost: $0 a month on a free Google account. Google’s limit: 100 email recipients a day per account, shared with booking (the form leaves 30 free for reminders); Google can change these limits without notice.
    • Safeguards: a honeypot field against bots, a brake if more than 20 complete inquiries arrive in an hour (it alerts you and visitors see the WhatsApp button), protection against malicious spreadsheet formulas, minimal permissions (only that spreadsheet and sending you the alert) and an off switch.
    • If it fails: the site tells the person to write on WhatsApp, and the WhatsApp button and email stay on the site.
    • What data it asks for: the minimum needed to reply (name, contact and message). It isn’t meant for sensitive data (health, ID numbers, cards, passwords); use another channel for that.

  • Booking

    Rest of the world: From USD 220Chile: From CLP 110,000 · 5 business days

    Before

    You arrange every appointment by message.

    After

    Your clients book on their own and get an email reminder.

    Technical details
    • What it uses: Google Calendar’s free booking page (one per personal account) plus a Google Apps Script that, around 6 pm each day, emails a reminder to whoever has an appointment the next day. The site links to the booking page (it doesn’t embed it).
    • Why a script and not Google’s own reminder: Google Calendar’s native automatic email reminder is a paid feature (Google One Premium, Workspace Individual and higher plans). The script replaces it on your free account.
    • Where the data lives: the appointment sits in your Google Calendar, with the booker’s name and email. The script keeps only the masked email (ju***@…) in its “Registro” tab, which clears itself after 60 days. The data doesn’t pass through any other third party.
    • Whose account: yours; you install it with your own login, guided by us. Nobody asks for your password.
    • Third-party cost: $0 a month on free accounts. Google’s limits: 100 email recipients a day (shared with the form) and 90 minutes a day of triggers; they can change without notice.
    • Safeguards: only appointments you created are reminded, with up to 3 guests; minimal permissions (read the calendar, write to that spreadsheet, send email) and an off switch for reminders.
    • What it’s for: booking appointments and reminding people by email. It asks only for name and email; it isn’t meant for health data or reasons for a visit (if you need clinical records, payments or WhatsApp reminders, you need a system built for that, and professional advice is wise). The booker’s email is not verified.
    • If it fails: Google alerts you and the script emails “Check tomorrow’s reminders”; the fallback is to notify people by WhatsApp by hand.

Questions

How long does it take?

One page, 10 business days; simple website, 20; custom, 30. The count starts when you send your texts and photos.

Who owns the site?

You do: files, domain and content, with documentation in plain language.

What isn’t included?

A logo with a drawn symbol, a photo shoot, an online store with payments, a blog or advertising.

Does the AI do it all on its own?

No. The agents do the heavy lifting in small steps and the developer reviews it with you before it's delivered.

Which AI do you use?

Our agents run on third-party AI models such as Claude. It isn't magic: with those same models you can build agents like these too. Several of our agents' prompts are open and free on our crew page.

What if I build it myself with AI?

You can, and for a simple page that may be enough. What usually gets left out is the rest of the road: deciding what to say and how to show it, publishing it in your name, checking security and accessibility, helping Google understand where you work, and, if you want, keeping it up to date. That’s what we do for you, so your time stays with your business.
